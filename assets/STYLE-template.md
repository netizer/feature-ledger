# The tone this ledger is written in

Replace everything below with your own. Write it as prose, the way you would
brief a new writer joining the team, not as a list of rules to be checked off.
An agent reads this and applies it; the more it reads like a specification,
the less like a voice it comes out.

It helps to say:

- Who reads this document, and what they already know.
- How formal it should feel.
- The words your product uses for its own things.
  ("The people who book are guests, never users or customers.")

## A sample in this tone

Then write one, and make it the centre of this file. A hundred words of prose
in the voice you want, describing a product — yours, or one you can describe
from memory. This is the part that does the work. An agent that reads a
paragraph in the right register writes in it; an agent that reads adjectives
about the register writes in its own.

If a well-known style guide is close to what you want, name it and say where
you differ. `ledger style list` shows the four that ship with the tool, and
`ledger style show` prints the sample each of them carries, which is a
reasonable place to start from.

## Three feature descriptions in this tone

Finish with three, each two or three sentences, describing one capability
apiece. This is the shape every entry in the ledger has to take, so three
worked ones carry further than any amount of instruction. Write them about
capabilities you know well enough to check.
